Lets a client say why they work with Oztix, in their words.

## Import

```tsx
import { Testimonial } from '@oztix/roadie-marketing-blocks/testimonial'
```

## Examples

### Quote

The quote sits large and centred under the heading, with the person's
portrait, name, role, and organisation under it. The quote and citation
share one `figure`, so they're read together. A `backgroundImage` fades
behind the whole block.

```tsx-live id=quote
<Testimonial
  preset='quote'
  title='Our partners say it best'
  description='Why venues and event organisers partner with Oztix'
  quote='Oztix are attuned to what we need and quick to respond, and their marketing sells tickets, plain and simple.'
  person={{
    name: 'Mia Tran',
    role: 'Head of Programming',
    image: { src: '/brand/photography/04.webp', alt: 'Portrait of Mia Tran', width: 120, height: 120 }
  }}
  organisation={{
    name: 'Thimble Orbit Presents',
    logo: { src: '/blocks/thimble-orbit-presents-badge.svg', alt: 'Thimble Orbit Presents logo', width: 120, height: 40 }
  }}
  backgroundImage={{ src: '/brand/photography/05.webp', alt: '', width: 1600, height: 900 }}
/>
```

## Guidelines

<Guidelines>

<Guideline title='Answer first'>
  <Guideline.Do>
    Pick a quote that says what Oztix did for them, such as selling more
    tickets, in its first sentence.
  </Guideline.Do>
  <Guideline.Dont>
    Don't open with praise that could be about anyone.
  </Guideline.Dont>
</Guideline>

<Guideline title='Keep it to two or three lines'>
  <Guideline.Do>
    Trim the quote to the sentence or two that carry it, with the person's
    agreement. The block draws the quotation marks.
  </Guideline.Do>
  <Guideline.Dont>
    Don't paste a whole review; at this size, a long quote reads as a wall.
  </Guideline.Dont>
</Guideline>

<Guideline title='Name a real person'>
  <Guideline.Do>
    Give the person's name, role, and organisation, with a square portrait
    and the organisation's logo on its own background, so it reads in both
    themes.
  </Guideline.Do>
  <Guideline.Dont>
    Don't quote "a happy client"; an unnamed quote doesn't persuade.
  </Guideline.Dont>
</Guideline>

</Guidelines>
