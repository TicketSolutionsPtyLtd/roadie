An AI draft breaks the same rules as any weak draft, and has a few habits of
its own. This page covers those habits, with a draft line before each edit
and after it. Edit everything else against
[Language and grammar](/content/language-and-grammar) and
[Voice and tone](/content/voice-and-tone), like any other draft.

## The tells

Each tell shows the draft line to avoid and the edit that fixes it.

<Guidelines>

<Guideline
  headingLevel={3}
  title="Trailing -ing phrases"
  description={`A clause like "highlighting its commitment" or "ensuring a smooth night", tacked onto the end of a sentence, claims a meaning nobody checked. Delete it, or give the fact behind it its own sentence.`}
>
  <Guideline.Do>

    The festival added a second stage for local acts.

    Gates open at 5pm, an hour earlier than last year.

  </Guideline.Do>
  <Guideline.Dont>

    The festival added a second stage, highlighting its commitment to local acts.

    Gates open at 5pm, ensuring a smooth entry for every fan.

  </Guideline.Dont>
</Guideline>

<Guideline
  headingLevel={3}
  title="AI vocabulary"
  description="Some words turn up far more often in machine writing than in ours. Use the plain word instead."
>
  <Guideline.Do>
    Here's how regional festivals sold this year.
  </Guideline.Do>
  <Guideline.Dont>
    Let's delve into the vibrant landscape of regional festivals.
  </Guideline.Dont>
</Guideline>

</Guidelines>

<PairTable>

| Write                   | Not                   |
| ----------------------- | --------------------- |
| also                    | additionally          |
| important, or say why   | crucial, pivotal      |
| look at                 | delve into            |
| show                    | showcase, underscore  |
| improve                 | enhance               |
| build                   | foster                |
| get                     | garner                |
| busy, loud, or the fact | vibrant               |
| market                  | landscape, as an idea |
| proof of                | testament to          |
| mix                     | tapestry, interplay   |

</PairTable>

<Guidelines>

<Guideline
  headingLevel={3}
  title="Not just this, but that"
  description={`"It's not just X, it's Y" sets up a claim nobody made so it can knock it down. Say the point.`}
>
  <Guideline.Do>
    We run your ticketing, and we answer the phone on show day.
  </Guideline.Do>
  <Guideline.Dont>
    It's not just a ticketing platform, it's a partner in your success.
  </Guideline.Dont>
</Guideline>

<Guideline
  headingLevel={3}
  title="Groups of three"
  description="List as many things as there are. A draft pads or trims ideas to three because three sounds finished."
>
  <Guideline.Do>
    Scanning takes 2 seconds a ticket.
  </Guideline.Do>
  <Guideline.Dont>
    Fast, simple, and seamless entry for every fan.
  </Guideline.Dont>
</Guideline>

<Guideline
  headingLevel={3}
  title="Bold labels that repeat the line"
  description="A bullet that opens with a bold label and a colon, then says the label again, is a tell. Write the sentence. A bold name that ends in a full stop and leads into new detail is fine, as in the second Do example."
>
  <Guideline.Do>

    Scanning took 2 seconds a ticket, down from 5.

    `**Refunds.** We refund to the card you paid with.`

  </Guideline.Do>
  <Guideline.Dont>

    `**Performance:** Performance improved across all venues.`

    `**Refunds:** Refunds are handled by our team.`

  </Guideline.Dont>
</Guideline>

</Guidelines>

### Curly quotes in drafts

Code, Markdown, and AI drafts use straight quotes, so search and code read
them. Rendered copy may use typographic quotes.

<PairTable>

| Before               | After                |
| -------------------- | -------------------- |
| `title=‘Doors open’` | `title='Doors open'` |

</PairTable>

<Guidelines>

<Guideline
  headingLevel={3}
  title="Chatbot phrases"
  description={`Delete the assistant's side of the conversation, such as "Certainly!", "I hope this helps!", and "Let me know if you have any questions". A warm sign-off is fine only after a concrete next step.`}
>
  <Guideline.Do>
    Gates open at 6pm. I'll send the run sheet by Thursday. Have a great show.
  </Guideline.Do>
  <Guideline.Dont>
    Certainly! Here's the update. I hope this helps!
  </Guideline.Dont>
</Guideline>

<Guideline
  headingLevel={3}
  title="Flattery"
  description="Answer the question. Don't praise it, or agree with the reader before you answer."
>
  <Guideline.Do>
    Refunds go back to the card you paid with within 5 business days.
  </Guideline.Do>
  <Guideline.Dont>
    Great question! You're absolutely right that refunds can be tricky.
  </Guideline.Dont>
</Guideline>

</Guidelines>

      <Callout intent='success' title='Great news!'>

      </Callout>
    }
  >
    Don't ship the draft's cheer or the clause it tacks on the end.
  </Guideline.Dont>
</Guideline>

</Guidelines>

## The rules for every writer

A draft also breaks the rules on
[Language and grammar](/content/language-and-grammar). These catch the most.

- [Clarity](/content/language-and-grammar#clarity) catches vague claims,
  inflated words, padding, hedging, unnamed sources, arrows, and fragments.
- [Use the plain word](/content/language-and-grammar#use-the-plain-word)
  lists the swaps for the most common inflated words.
- [Dashes](/content/language-and-grammar#dashes) bans em dashes and en dashes.
