Oztix is Australia's largest independent ticketing company. These pages are for
designers, agencies, and partners making anything that carries the Oztix
brand, such as posters, social posts, emails, decks, and print.

Roadie is where the brand meets code. The brand's colours are Roadie's colour
scales, its type is Roadie's text styles, and its spot illustrations are Roadie
components. A design and the app it becomes share one source.

## How the brand comes together

Oztix work combines a small set of elements the same way every time. A poster,
a social post, an email, and a deck all build from the same five steps.

### 1. Colour sets the stage

Most designs sit on white, a light neutral, or one of the house colours. House
blue is the most common brand background. House purple suits music and venues,
and house orange suits shows and sports.

Spotlight blue is the lead brand colour, but it appears in small, concentrated
moments, such as buttons, icons, and accents. It isn't a large background,
except as the ground under the pattern's house colour curves.
[Colour](/brand/colour) has the full palette.

### 2. Type carries the weight

Headings are set in Intermission, bold and in sentence case. Large, heavy
headings do the work, and lighter weights carry the supporting copy. Don't add
other typefaces.

A key heading or call to action often sits in a house colour block. The block
gives the eye a focal point and anchors the layout.

### 3. Illustration or pattern adds texture

Illustration and pattern give Oztix work its character, so most designs use
one or the other, and sometimes both.

- Illustrations are dimensional, conceptual graphics that tell a story. Use
  them for hero moments, campaigns, and anywhere a strong visual idea is
  needed.
- The pattern is a flowing swirl that adds energy and texture without
  competing with the content. Use it for backgrounds, overlays, and frames.

Scale either one to sit with the heading, not compete with it.

### 4. Photography grounds it in reality

Keep photography authentic, with real events, real crowds, and real moments.

### 5. The logo anchors it

Place the logo last, once everything else is in place. Use the primary logo
with the tagline wherever there's room.

## A typical layout

A well composed Oztix piece usually has these parts.

- A neutral or house colour background.
- A bold Intermission heading, often in a house colour block.
- An illustration or the pattern, scaled to sit with the heading.
- One main idea, supported by minimal copy.
- The Oztix logo, with clear space around it.

Colour, bold type, and an illustration or the pattern together are the
signature. Leave one out and the work feels unfinished.

## Tone

Oztix sounds like an experienced partner, not a corporate vendor. Copy is
direct, active, and grounded in real outcomes. Write in sentence case and
Australian English, keep it active, and cut the filler. Write "use", not
"utilise".
